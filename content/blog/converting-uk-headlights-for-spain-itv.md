Of all the jobs involved in getting a UK car onto Spanish plates, the headlights catch more people out than anything else. Get them wrong and the car fails its import ITV. Leave them until the last minute and you can find yourself waiting weeks for parts.

Here's what the inspection looks for, and how to get your lights ready.

## Why UK headlights are a problem

Headlights don't just point straight ahead. Their dipped beam has a deliberate shape: it throws more light towards the kerb and cuts off sharply on the side of oncoming traffic, so you see the road edge without dazzling other drivers.

UK headlights are designed for driving on the **left**, so they dip towards the left-hand kerb. In Spain, where you drive on the right, that same beam shines straight into the eyes of oncoming drivers.

The import ITV checks for a beam pattern **made for driving on the right**. A UK beam pattern won't pass.

## What about beam deflector stickers?

The stickers you buy for a summer driving holiday in Europe mask part of the lens to stop you dazzling people. They're meant for temporary trips and aren't a fix for a car being registered here permanently. Plan on a proper conversion.

## Your options, by type of headlight

The right solution depends on the lamp. Look at the label on top of the headlight unit, or send us a photo of one headlight and its label, and we can tell you which applies.

### Halogen

Older and simpler cars usually have halogen headlights. The most common fix is to fit **replacement headlight units made for right-hand traffic**, carrying the correct E-mark. For popular models these are often easy to find. For less common cars they may need ordering in, which is why it pays to start early.

### Xenon and LED

Many modern xenon and LED lights can be **adjusted for right-hand traffic**, sometimes with a setting inside the unit and sometimes through the car's software. Where the existing lamps can't be adjusted, the alternative is replacement units built for right-hand traffic, which can be expensive for premium cars.

### Already set for driving on the right

Some cars, such as those originally built for the European market, already have the right beam pattern. Check before you spend any money.

## Don't forget the rear fog light

UK cars often have a single rear fog light on the side that suits driving on the left. Spanish rules expect it on the side for driving on the right, so the fog light may need **moving** before the ITV. It's a small job, but an easy one to forget.

## Other things the ITV checks

The import ITV is a thorough inspection and takes about an hour and a half. Besides the lights, it checks the car against its technical documents and looks at things like tyres, brakes, emissions and the condition of the car. We check tyres and lights before booking the test, so there are no surprises on the day.

If the car does fail, the inspector lists what needs fixing. We tell you the price of any extra work before doing it, fix it, and take the car back for the re-test.
